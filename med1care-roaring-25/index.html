Option A — The Marquee

Anniversary emblem

Client-gift energy

A single arched plaque holds the wordmark, ROARING, the big 25th and the tagline in one clean badge. Deco details (sunburst, diamond-ended rules) nod to the original Victorian idea but at weights that actually print. This is the one to hand a patient or partner — it looks like a commemorative object, not promo merch.

Option B — The Roar

Block type

Loud & legible

Condensed block caps stacked tight: wordmark, ROARING, a hero 25th, then the tagline. Zero decoration, maximum contrast. Reads from across a lobby, photographs well, and prints with the fewest possible screen issues — the safest bet if the tee is going to a big, mixed audience.

What changed, and why

  • Killed the hairlines. The proof's concentric rings, dotted rules and scroll curls are 1–2 pt at size. On a single screen they bridge and fill in, or vanish. Everything here is at least 3 pt with open counters.
  • One consistent type family. The original mixed a spur-heavy display face, a script "Care" and faux-ribbon warping. That reads as five logos fighting. Now it's one system: deco light caps, a heavy numeral, and the real Med1Care mark.
  • The logo leads. Med1Care (with its boxed, flagged 1) is the first thing you read on both fronts, set as live vector from the client's own construction.
  • The back was rebuilt. The proof crammed "MED1CARE" vertically into a 4″ sliver so each letter became a bar. Here the spine mark runs at a readable width with upright letters, plus a small nape hit so the shirt still says Roaring 25 when someone's facing away.
  • Dropped "Merry Christmas." A 25-year anniversary gets worn in June, at hiring events, at fundraisers. A seasonal line dates the garment — happy to set a small Est. 2001 or the anniversary year instead if you want a fourth line.
  • Type is outlined. No live text in the files — the printer can't re-flow, substitute or re-space a word, which is where most proofs go sideways.
One decision for you: both backs keep the 4″ × 15″ spine placement from the Jupmode spec. If you'd rather the back be a full-width horizontal lockup, that's a 2-minute swap.
